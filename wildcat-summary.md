## The wildcat: restored by protecting it, not by releasing it

The European wildcat (*Felis silvestris*) is the Côa's mid-sized predator. GBIF holds no wildcat record in the study area, and the project treats the species as sensitive: every record is generalised to a 5 km cell before it reaches a map. Restoring the wildcat here means three things, none of them a release:

(i) **Keep it wild.** Hybridisation with domestic cats is the main threat, with persecution. The measure is neutering of domestic cats near villages.

(ii) **Restore its prey.** The wildcat depends on the European rabbit, so rabbit release candidates were screened only inside the top third of the wildcat's land corridors: 1,045 km² in ten priority blocks.

(iii) **Watch the mesopredators.** Where rabbits crash, foxes and cats switch to other prey, reptiles included (Derham et al., 2018; Finlayson et al., 2021). In Spain, the lynx's return cut fox and mongoose numbers (Jiménez et al., 2019). The Côa has no lynx, so the question is whether a recovering wildcat can hold part of that role (H2: wildcat habitat tracks rabbit habitat).

Who counts the wildcat in a valley where it leaves no public record?
