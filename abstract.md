**Title**: Rabbits and wildcats in the Greater Côa Valley: an open connectivity screen for trophic rewilding in northern Portugal

**Format**: Poster. **Series**: Lands in Motion.

**Author**: Linda Angulo Lopez, Rewilding Portugal. ORCID 0009-0009-5689-7148.

## Abstract

The European rabbit is the keystone prey of the Iberian Peninsula and Endangered in its native range; the European wildcat depends on it. We asked whether the rabbit can be restored inside the corridors the wildcat already uses in the Greater Côa Valley, northern Portugal, a rewilding landscape whose river stayed free-flowing after a 1995 civic campaign, "As gravuras não sabem nadar", stopped a dam to save its Palaeolithic rock art. We tested three hypotheses from a published synthesis: rabbits follow the grazing mosaic (H1), wildcat habitat tracks rabbit habitat (H2), and fire resets the rabbit mosaic (H3). An open, laptop-scale connectivity workflow (10,230 km² on a 100 m grid, ten focal species, circuit theory in pure Python, with penalties for fire, 22 field-recorded barriers and two UNESCO World Heritage areas) gave the land corridors of the species group that includes the wildcat. We screened rabbit habitat as scrub-pasture mosaic from ESA WorldCover, not burned since 2023 and away from roads, and kept only blocks inside the top third of those corridors. Rabbit mosaic covers 4,410 km², but only 1,045 km² of it lies inside the corridors, in ten priority survey blocks, and 87% of that is on hunting land. Recent fire removed little habitat, so H3 cannot yet be tested. GBIF holds no wildcat record in the study area. The restoration matters for Natura 2000: the network covers 26% of the modelled area but only 12% of the wildcat's top corridors, so the links between sites lie on hunting land outside them, and four of the five leading blocks sit within 4 km of the Serra da Estrela and Malcata sites, on the Sistema Central axis that continues into Spain, where the model stops. We propose a rabbit baseline run with hunters, neutering of domestic cats and mesopredator monitoring before any rabbit release, camera traps on a 5 km grid to test H2, and extending the model into Spain. Which predators will the next chapter of the Côa include, and who decides?

**Keywords**: rewilding; European rabbit; European wildcat; mesopredators; ecological connectivity; circuit theory; hunting land; Natura 2000; transboundary conservation; open science; Portugal
